\begin{abstract}
Emergency responders need fire fronts and high-priority areas to be inspected again and again,
but a small fleet of battery-limited drones must also return to share a single charger. We
model this as a fully observable, finite-horizon Markov decision process in which one central
controller chooses a joint move for three drones on a 40$\times$40 grid with stochastic fire
spread and changing wind. Three reinforcement learning (RL) agents were implemented and
compared: a deep Q-network (DQN), REINFORCE with a learned baseline, and synchronous advantage
actor--critic (A2C). Each was trained with three random seeds and evaluated on 20 unseen
worlds against a one-step Greedy controller, a rolling-horizon planner, a lawnmower sweep and
random policies. After a feasibility mask was added, every learned policy kept all drones
alive, but inspection quality stayed at the level of random feasible actions: weighted
freshness of 0.084 (DQN), 0.069 (REINFORCE) and 0.084 (A2C) against 0.084 for masked random
and 0.221 for Greedy. A learning-rate sweep and a sensitivity study did not change this
picture. Measurements show that the immediate reward separates good and bad joint actions by
far less than the noise in mission returns, which explains why none of the agents learned
coordinated patrols. We propose factorised credit assignment, potential-based shaping and
learning from demonstrations as improvements, and partial observability, decentralised
communication and calibrated fire physics as extensions.
\end{abstract}
